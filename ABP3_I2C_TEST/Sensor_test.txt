Yes. At this point, we’ve actually achieved **quite a lot**. I’d divide the work into **confirmed**, **strongly demonstrated**, and **still pending**.

## ✅ What we have achieved

### 1. ESP32 ↔ Honeywell sensor communication is working

We successfully established I²C communication between:

* **ESP32**
* SDA → GPIO 21
* SCL → GPIO 22
* Sensor address → **0x6C**
* I²C clock → **100 kHz**

The sensor is responding consistently and giving valid data frames.

So we can confidently say:

> **The ESP32 is communicating with the sensor correctly over I²C.**

Honeywell's digital pressure sensors use I²C communication with pull-ups on SDA/SCL. ([Honeywell][1])

---

### 2. We figured out the correct measurement sequence

Our code is successfully doing the sensor's single-shot measurement sequence:

**Trigger measurement → wait → read temperature + pressure + status**

We're getting the expected six-byte data structure:

```text
Temperature
Pressure
Status
```

and we're interpreting the 16-bit pressure value as a signed two's-complement number.

That's a major milestone because we're no longer just seeing random bytes—we're interpreting the sensor's output according to the protocol.

---

### 3. Temperature measurement works

We've consistently obtained temperatures around:

```text
~29.5 – 30.7 °C
```

For example, the initial reading was approximately:

```text
Temperature Counts : -5029
Temperature         : 29.53 °C
```

And during the pressure experiments, temperature remained relatively stable around 30 °C.

So:

> **Temperature acquisition and conversion are working.**

### 5. We demonstrated that SENSE+ and SENSE− are actually being used as differential ports

We performed the equal-pressure test by connecting the two pneumatic ports together.

The sensor settled around:

```text
~ -59 counts average
```

with a relatively small variation.

That tells us the sensor behaves sensibly when the pressure difference between the two ports is approximately zero.

And importantly:

**SENSE+ and SENSE− are pneumatic pressure ports, not electrical connections.**

With approximately equal pressure on both sides, we observed a baseline around:

```text
~ -60 counts
```

rather than something wildly unstable.

So we now have an experimentally observed **zero-differential offset**.

However, we have **not yet officially calibrated it away**.

That's intentional—we shouldn't hard-code the offset until we finish the pressure calibration.

### 8. We checked the sensor's status information

We've consistently been seeing statuses around:

```text
0xC019
```

and decoded the relevant diagnostic bits.

Most importantly, we're **not seeing indications of**:

* CRC error

* bridge check failure
* bridge supply failure

during the pressure experiments.

So there is currently **no strong evidence that the sensor itself is malfunctioning**.

### 9. We verified that the raw data behaves consistently with the physical experiment

This is an important engineering point.

We're not relying on the displayed:

```text
Pressure : xx kPa
```

to prove anything yet.

We're looking at the **raw signed pressure counts**.

And those counts correlate with what you're physically doing:

```text
No/low differential pressure
        ↓
   low counts
        ↓
Apply pressure
        ↓
counts increase progressively
        ↓
Hold pressure
        ↓
counts remain relatively high
        ↓
Release pressure
        ↓
counts decrease progressively
```

That is exactly the type of behavior we wanted to see.

# ⚠️ What we have NOT achieved yet

There are two major things left.

### 1. Exact pressure calibration

This is the biggest remaining task.

Right now we've been temporarily displaying pressure using:

```text
Pressure = counts / 25600
```

under the assumption of a particular ±1 kPa / ±25600-count configuration.

**We have NOT proven that this is the correct scale for your exact sensor.**

Honeywell's ABP family has many different pressure ranges/configurations, so we shouldn't assume the range from the physical marking alone. ([Honeywell Automation][2])

Therefore:

> **The raw counts are trustworthy; the current kPa number is provisional.**

---

### 2. We haven't yet established the polarity with a controlled known pressure

We have evidence that increasing pressure on SENSE+ increases the counts.

But we haven't yet performed a proper controlled test like:

```text
0 Pa      → X counts
+100 Pa   → Y counts
+200 Pa   → Z counts
+500 Pa   → W counts
```

with a known reference pressure.

Once we do that, we'll be able to determine:

* zero offset
* counts/Pa
* Pa/count
* actual pressure range
* polarity
* linearity
* repeatability
